# Working with Ayush on this repo

## How to explain things — this is the most important rule here

**One idea per turn. Then stop and check.**

Not "one topic with five sub-points". One idea. Then ask whether it landed,
and wait for the answer before adding the next one.

- **Why before what.** The reason a thing matters comes before the thing itself.
- **No parallel findings.** If two things are true, say the more important one and
  hold the other. Say "there's a second thing, want it now or later?"
- **No preemptive caveats.** Do not attach the trap, the exception and the
  methodology note to a finding he has not yet agreed with. They are their own
  turns.
- **Plain words first, numbers second.** A table is a follow-up, not an opener.

**Signals he is lost — stop adding, go back and simplify:**
"not clear", "I didn't understand", "what?!", "too much", "?!" .
The correct response is a SHORTER message than the last one, not a
re-explanation with more scaffolding. Cut to one sentence if needed.

**He is a hardware engineer and this is his MSc thesis.** He is not a beginner —
the problem is never that the material is too advanced, it is that too much of it
arrives at once. Depth is welcome; volume is not.

## Other standing preferences

- **Give him commands to run**, don't run builds and simulations via Bash.
- **The sequencer and the instructions are HIS contribution.** Explain the
  architecture; write the gem5 framework plumbing for him and label it "skip".
- **Verify before presenting.** Never offer a design option as viable until it has
  been traced through real code. An in-code comment is not evidence.
- **Terse code comments**, one-liners, not multi-line blocks.
- **Faithful model only.** No CPU-side magic-state shortcuts, even as throwaways.
